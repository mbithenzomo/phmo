# Personal Health Monitoring Ontology (PHMO)

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24155503.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24155503)

The Personal Health Monitoring Ontology (PHMO) is a reusable general ontology for AI-based personal health monitoring applications. It supports the representation of system users, sensor data, medical conditions, health risks, health events, interventions, AI models, datasets, evidence, and explanations. The PHMO defines how these concepts are modelled. Condition-specific ontologies can extend the PHMO and define which concepts are modelled for a given condition. For example, the [Atrial Fibrillation Monitoring Ontology (AFMO)](https://mbithenzomo.github.io/afmo/) extends the PHMO for atrial fibrillation.

The PHMO supports three main functionalities: health risk assessment, health event prediction, and health intervention recommendation. Each output is generated by an AI model, such as a Bayesian network or a neural network. A health risk assessment estimates the probability that a monitored person will develop a medical condition. A health event prediction estimates the probability that a health event will occur within a short time window. A health intervention recommendation proposes a medical action, with an expected utility calculated by a Bayesian decision network. Each output is linked to the model that generated it, the inputs it was based on, and the datasets used to train and test the model. Health risk relationships and health interventions are also linked to supporting scientific evidence, and each piece of evidence is linked to its publication. This allows system recommendations to be grounded in, and explained through, the scientific literature.

To support interoperability, the PHMO reuses concepts from established ontologies, including the Sensor, Observation, Sample, and Actuator (SOSA) ontology, the Smart Applications REFerence (SAREF) ontology, the Evidence and Conclusion Ontology (ECO), the FRBR-aligned Bibliographic Ontology (FaBiO), and the Explanation Ontology (EO).

## Scope

The PHMO covers the following aspects of a personal health monitoring system:

- **System users:** monitored persons, clinicians, and caregivers, and the relationships between them.
- **Sensor data:** health devices and sensors, the properties they observe, and their observations. Discrete observations (such as height and weight) and continuous observations (such as biosignal recordings) are both supported. Raw time series data is not stored in the ontology; continuous observations link to an external data store instead.
- **Health risks:** risk factor categories, and the relationships between a medical condition and risk factor categories, risk factors, and risk factor states. These relationships carry category weights, factor weights, and state risks, and support effect modification.
- **Risk assessments and event predictions:** system outputs made by models, with their probability, status, timestamp, and inputs. Event predictions also record their input window, target window, and prediction horizon.
- **Models:** the models behind each output, their performance metrics, decision thresholds, and training and test datasets.
- **Health interventions:** recommended medical actions, their expected utility, and clinician approval (pending, approved, pre-approved, or rejected).
- **Evidence:** scientific evidence and publications supporting risk relationships and interventions.
- **Explanations:** explanations of system recommendations, and the users who receive them.

Version 1.0 defines 38 classes, 84 object properties, 31 data properties, and 26 individuals in the `phmo` namespace.

## Reused ontologies

The PHMO reuses concepts from the following ontologies:

- Sensor, Observation, Sample, and Actuator (SOSA) Ontology
- Smart Applications REFerence (SAREF) Core Ontology
- SAREF4Wear Ontology
- SAREF4EHAW (eHealth and Ageing Well) Ontology
- Evidence and Conclusion Ontology (ECO)
- Medical Action Ontology (MAxO)
- FRBR-aligned Bibliographic Ontology (FaBiO)
- Friend of a Friend (FOAF) Ontology
- Data Catalog Vocabulary (DCAT)
- Explanation Ontology (EO) and Explanation Patterns Ontology (EP)

Metadata terms are reused from the Dublin Core Metadata Initiative (DCMI) Metadata Terms and the PRISM Basic Metadata Specification.

Health domain concepts have been manually cross-referenced to concepts from the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) metathesaurus.

## Extending the PHMO

To build an ontology for a specific medical condition:

1. Import the PHMO, preferably by its version IRI, so that your ontology is tied to a tested release:

   ```turtle
   <http://example.org/my-ontology> owl:imports <http://purl.org/phmo/1.0> .
   ```

2. Add the medical condition and its comorbidities as subclasses of `phmo:MedicalCondition`.
3. Add the risk factors selected for the condition as subclasses of the PHMO risk factor categories, such as `phmo:DemographicFactor` or `phmo:LifestyleFactor`.
4. Add condition-specific health events, observation types, and devices as subclasses of the corresponding PHMO classes.

See the [Atrial Fibrillation Monitoring Ontology (AFMO)](https://mbithenzomo.github.io/afmo/) as an example.

## Version history

- **1.0:** first release. The PHMO was abstracted from the Atrial Fibrillation Monitoring Ontology (AFMO), which was originally developed as a single application ontology. General personal health monitoring concepts were moved from the `afmo` namespace to the `phmo` namespace.
